How to Generate App Store Screenshots: Step-by-Step Guide

Below are the 9 steps that will help you to generate app store screenshots with an iPhone screenshot template in AppLaunchpad. The whole process takes around 30 minutes.

1. Create a Free Account — Go to theapplaunchpad.com and sign up with your email or Google account to create a free account. Once inside, click "Create a design" from your dashboard. This opens the template gallery where you can browse iPhone screenshot templates.

2. Choose an iPhone Pre-Built Template — Filter the template gallery by iPhone to see all available iPhone screenshot templates. The default size is iPhone 6.9", which matches the latest iPhone models and meets current App Store requirements. Pick a template that fits your app's style, then click "Use This Template" to load it in the editor. For a different iPhone size, click "More Screenshot Sizes" inside the editor to pick any supported model, from the iPhone SE to the latest iPhone Pro Max — choosing the correct size upfront helps prevent rejection errors in App Store Connect.

3. Upload Your App Screenshots — Click on the device frame in the editor, and the upload button will appear. Click it and select your screenshot from your computer. No need to worry about the screenshot size, since AppLaunchpad will automatically fit it into the device frame. Make sure to use high-resolution PNG files exported from your app or Xcode simulator, and try to avoid blurry screenshots.

4. Add a Caption — Click the text element in the template to edit it. Replace the placeholder with your own caption. The best captions are short, specific, and benefit-driven.

Good caption examples:

  • "Track habits in under 10 seconds"
  • "Never miss a payment again."
  • "Your finances, finally organised."

If a text element doesn't fit your design, delete it entirely. Not every template slot needs to be filled — less clutter means more focus on the app.

5. Style Your Caption — Once your captions are in place, style them: font family (standard on Free, 500+ premium fonts on Pro), font size, text colour to match your brand palette, alignment, and bold/italic sparingly to stress key words. A strong, readable caption on a well-chosen template grabs attention before the viewer even looks at the screen inside the device frame.

6. Customise the Background — The background ties your entire app store screenshot together. You get three options: a solid colour (clean and minimal), a gradient (smooth blend between two colours), or a custom image upload for a fully customised look. Use brand colours from your app's design system and keep them consistent across all screenshots.

7. Add Elements — The Elements tab gives you a full library of design extras: 2,000+ icons to match your app's theme, shapes and frames like arrows and borders, and 10,000+ illustrations on Pro. Drag any element onto the canvas and try not to overload the screen — one or two strong elements per screenshot is usually enough.

8. Add Custom Images — Besides the built-in elements, you can upload your own images directly into the editor — your app icon, product photo, mascot, or any branded visual. PNG with a transparent background works best since it overlays cleanly without needing a white box around it.

9. Preview and Download Your Screenshots — Preview all your screenshots side by side and check that font, colour, and layout stay consistent across every slide. Click "Download" to get your screenshots as JPEG or PNG — AppLaunchpad auto-generates all required App Store sizes in one download. Download as PNG for the sharpest quality, or JPEG if file size matters.

Before you export, run a quick final check. Generate screenshots for your three most important features first, build those until they're tight, then expand to a full set of 5 to 8 slides.

Tips to Make Your App Store Screenshots Convert

  • Lead with your strongest feature. Your first screenshot should immediately answer "What does this app do?" Don't save the best for slide 3.
  • Run variations. AppLaunchpad lets you duplicate a project and swap captions or backgrounds in minutes — test two versions of your first screenshot with the same screen but a different headline.
  • Refresh your template after every major update. Outdated screens signal an unmaintained app to anyone browsing the store.
  • Write captions as outcomes, not features. "Never miss a bill" beats "Bill reminder feature."
  • Keep the visual style consistent. Same font, same background palette, same layout logic across all screenshots.
  • Use the template as a guide, not a cage. Drag, resize, and adjust everything to make it yours.
  • Test your set before submitting. High-resolution, high-quality screenshots improve conversion rates significantly, and AppLaunchpad ensures every export meets the required resolution automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How Many App Store Screenshots Do I Need for iPhone?

Apple requires a minimum of 1 screenshot but recommends using all 10 available slots. Most top-performing apps use 5 to 8 screenshots, each highlighting a distinct feature or benefit.

2. What Size Should App Store Screenshots Be for iPhone?

For the latest iPhones, the required size is 1290 x 2796 pixels (iPhone 6.9"). AppLaunchpad handles this automatically, so there's no need for manual resizing.

3. Can I Use AppLaunchpad for Both App Store and Google Play?

Yes. AppLaunchpad supports both iOS and Android, and you can switch between device types in the editor and export for both platforms from a single project.

4. Is AppLaunchpad Free to Use?

Yes — the free plan includes 10 iPhone screenshot templates and 100+ graphics. Full access to 500+ templates and 10,000+ design assets starts at ₹650/month on the Pro plan. No credit card is needed to try the free plan.

5. Can I Localise My App Store Screenshots Into Multiple Languages?

Yes, localisation is a Pro plan feature. AppLaunchpad uses AI to translate caption text and automatically adjusts the layout for different language lengths.

6. Do I Need Design Skills to Use AppLaunchpad?

No. The template library and drag-and-drop editor mean you can go from blank canvas to download-ready screenshots without any graphic design knowledge.
